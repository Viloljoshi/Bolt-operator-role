01 / Why me

Three years of doing exactly this, minus the title.

The posting describes a builder-operator who owns a problem end to end and gets AI into live operations largely on their own. That has been the actual job for the last three years, in places where a wrong answer is expensive.

The build gets shipped, not handed off

Taking a problem on means writing the agent, the SQL, and the API calls, then putting it in front of operators directly. A rough tool that runs this month beats a polished plan still waiting on someone else's sprint. Closing that gap is the whole reason builder-operator roles exist.

No waiting on a team to free up

Internal data, APIs, LLMs, automation frameworks: enough to get a usable tool live without booking Engineering or Data for weeks. They get pulled in when it genuinely helps scale, not to get version one out the door.

Every build carries a number

Cost, reliability, and throughput matter more here than how many features went out. The work worth showing comes with figures attached: 45% less manual review, 70% fewer manual ops, 32% fewer false positives. Those numbers decide what gets built next, and they are what goes up to leadership.

Guardrails go in first

Years in banking and compliance, where an AI decision nobody can explain becomes a problem fast, made this a habit. Evaluation, monitoring, and clear fallbacks go in early. By the time something reaches production it is already auditable, and operators trust it because they can see why it did what it did.

An agent without guardrails is just an expensive bug. The part that earns trust is the unglamorous one: the monitoring, the evaluation, the fallback paths. It is the 80% nobody puts in a demo, and the reason operators rely on a tool instead of working around it.

A working principle for every system shipped

04 / First 90 days

Earning trust early.

The plan is to ship something real quickly and compound from there, with specifics rather than ambition.

Days 0 to 30 · Listen, ship small

Map the friction, ship one tool

  • Sit with ops teams and inventory the manual workarounds and recurring exceptions
  • Get hands on the internal data, APIs, and tooling that already exist
  • Ship one small, real automation or dashboard to build some credibility
Days 30 to 60 · Build the first agent

Pick the worst problem, prototype to pilot

  • Prioritise by cost, efficiency, and reliability impact, with a short decision memo
  • Build an AI agent or decision tool, with evaluation and monitoring beside it
  • Pilot in a single market with human checkpoints in the loop
Days 60 to 90 · Roll out, govern

Scale what works, set the guardrails

  • Roll the pilot to more markets, track adoption, and keep iterating
  • Define the governance baseline: explainability, evaluation gates, fallbacks
  • Publish a prioritised build backlog with quantified ROI for the next quarter
