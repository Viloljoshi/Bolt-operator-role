01 / Why me

This is the role I've been doing without the title.

The posting describes a builder-operator who owns a problem end to end and gets AI into live operations largely on their own. That has been my actual job for the last three years, in places where a wrong answer is expensive.

I ship what I build

When I take a problem on, I write the agent, the SQL, and the API calls, then I put it in front of operators myself. A rough tool that runs this month beats a polished plan still waiting on someone else's sprint. Closing that gap is the whole reason builder-operator roles exist.

I don't wait for a team to free up

Internal data, APIs, LLMs, automation frameworks: I can get a usable tool live without booking Engineering or Data for weeks. I pull them in when it genuinely helps me scale, not to get version one out the door.

Every build has a number on it

I pay more attention to cost, reliability, and throughput than to how many features went out. The work I'm proud of comes with figures attached: 45% less manual review, 70% fewer manual ops, 32% fewer false positives. Those numbers are how I decide what to build next and how I report it upward.

I put the guardrails in first

I spent years in banking and compliance, where an AI decision nobody can explain becomes a problem fast. So evaluation, monitoring, and clear fallbacks go in early. By the time something reaches production it is already auditable, and operators trust it because they can see why it did what it did.

An agent without guardrails is just an expensive bug. The part that earns trust is the unglamorous one: the monitoring, the evaluation, the fallback paths. It's the 80% nobody puts in a demo, and it's why operators rely on a tool instead of working around it.

On one platform that work moved adoption from 25% to 41% and supported a $15M pipeline. 04 / First 90 days

How I'd earn trust early.

The plan is to ship something real quickly and compound from there, with specifics rather than ambition.

Map the friction, ship one tool

  • Get hands on the internal data, APIs, and tooling that already exist
  • Ship one small, real automation or dashboard to build some credibility
Days 30 to 60 · Build the first agent

Pick the worst problem, prototype to pilot

  • Prioritise by cost, efficiency, and reliability impact, with a short decision memo
  • Build an AI agent or decision tool and stand up evaluation and monitoring beside it
  • Pilot in a single market with human checkpoints in the loop
Days 60 to 90 · Roll out, govern

Scale what works, set the guardrails

  • Roll the pilot to more markets, track adoption, and keep iterating
  • Define the governance baseline: explainability, evaluation gates, fallbacks
  • Publish a prioritised build backlog with quantified ROI for the next quarter
